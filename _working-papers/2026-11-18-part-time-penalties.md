**Revise and resubmit** at *Macroeconomic Dynamics*.

**Abstract:**
Older male workers exhibit substantial heterogeneity in retirement behavior across occupations and respond differently to retirement policy reforms. A key source of this heterogeneity is the part-time penalty—the reduction in hourly wages when workers switch from full-time to part-time employment. Workers in occupations with higher part-time penalties are more likely to retire earlier, as observed in data from Japan and the United States. To explain this pattern, I develop a general equilibrium model, demonstrating that greater asset accumulation and more generous pension benefits raise reservation wages and contribute to this earlier retirement. Using the Japanese Panel Study of Employment Dynamics, I show that standard retirement policy reforms—eliminating the earnings test, extending the pension eligibility age, and reducing pension benefits—have modest or negative effects on aggregate effective labor and output. In contrast, expanding income tax credits and exempting pension benefits from income taxation increase aggregate effective labor by about 1% across occupations, raise output by 1.197% and 0.5492%, respectively, and improve long-term welfare.
